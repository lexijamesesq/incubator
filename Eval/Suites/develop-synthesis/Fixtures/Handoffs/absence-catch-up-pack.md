# Research Handoff: absence-catch-up-pack

**Date:** 2026-11-10
**Idea:** Absence Catch-Up Pack
**Seed core insight:** Coursebook gives a returning student no single place to see what they missed, so each teacher rebuilds it by hand for each absence.
**Seed strategic connection:** Supports the family-engagement measure by giving parents a clear list of the work their child missed.

---

## Strategic Context (Stream A)

### Product Strategy — Direct

- The No Lost Week pillar reads: "when a routine breaks, the learner should lose the day, not the week." It lists three breaks in order: a student's absence, a teacher's absence, a school closure. Return from absence is the first. ([Larkmoor Product Strategy 2026, No Lost Week](https://example.com/larkmoor-product-strategy-2026-no-lost-week))

### Design Strategy — Adjacent

- Fewer Tabs is about teachers switching tools. A pack removes the paper tray and the re-posted feed message, which are not tabs.

### OKR Connections

- Measure 2a (families opted in to school messages): Weak. The seed connects here. The measure counts message opt-ins; a pack is read by the student and is not a message to a family.
- Measure 3b (students returning to Coursebook each week): Adjacent. A student back from absence has a reason to open Coursebook on the first day back.

### NPS / Customer Evidence

- Larkmoor Coursebook (April–September 2026): Zero mentions of absence or catch-up. 31 teacher comments describe re-posting instructions "for the kids who were out". ([Coursebook NPS — September 2026 tab](https://example.com/coursebook-nps))
- **Key finding:** Teachers describe the workaround and do not ask for a fix.

### Shared Research Baseline (within TTL)

- Teachers spend a median 25 minutes a week per absent student assembling missed work, and 38% of students absent three days or more never complete it. (high) ([Fernhollow Institute, Absence and Recovery Study 2025](https://example.com/fernhollow-absence-recovery-2025))
- 19% of basin students missed a tenth or more of the 2025–26 school year. (high) ([Basin Education Authority, Attendance Bulletin 2026](https://example.com/bea-attendance-bulletin-2026))

## Market Intelligence (Stream B — EdTech SME)

### Market Fit: Strong

Districts have a dated obligation and are buying against it. The Attendance Recovery Directive takes effect August 2027 and requires a district to document the "learning continuity provision" made for every absence of three days or more ([Basin Education Authority, Attendance Recovery Directive guidance](https://example.com/bea-attendance-recovery-directive)). 57 of 212 basin district RFPs issued in 2025–26 ask for absence-continuity features ([Ledgerline RFP Index 2026](https://example.com/ledgerline-rfp-index-2026)). The buyer is the district attendance lead, and the demand is written down.

### Competitive Positioning

| Competitor | Relevant Capability | Comparison |
| ------------ | --------------------- | ------------ |
| Corvid Classroom (registry) | "Missed Work" filter listing a student's unsubmitted assignments by date, shipped September 2025; no lesson content | Behind |
| Mossgate Labs (discovered) | Standalone lesson summariser; the teacher pastes in notes for an absent student; 45,000 teacher accounts | Differentiation |
| Pennywhistle Assess (registry) | Nothing comparable | Differentiation |

**Positioning narrative:** Larkmoor is behind Corvid on the simple list and nobody assembles what a class did on the missed days. Corvid's product lead said in an October 2026 webinar that "catch-up bundles" are planned for 2027 (no public link).

### Technology Risk: Medium

Coursebook knows what was posted on a given day. It does not know what was said in the room or which posted items mattered. The Learner Data Charter restricts absence reasons, so a pack must not reveal why a student was out.

### Strategic Timing

About nine months: the Directive applies from August 2027 and Corvid has announced for the same year.

## Educator Perspective (Stream D)

### Adoption Assessment: Medium

Teachers would switch it on tomorrow. Whether a returning fourteen-year-old opens it is the real question, and nothing here answers it.

### Pain Point Validity

Felt every morning by the teacher: the same "what did I miss?" from five students before the bell. For the student the pain is real and unspoken; they stop asking by the third teacher.

### Adoption Barriers

A pack that lists everything posted on four days is a wall. It is useful only if the teacher marks which two or three items matter, and that is a new step on a day they are already behind. Students who were out for a week are the least likely to go looking.

### Champion Scenario

Monday, a Year 9 student back after four days opens one page showing three items marked essential across six subjects and finishes two in a study period, instead of queuing at six desks.

## Divergent Reframing (Stream E)

### The Disruptive Angle: The pack belongs to a classmate

Give the pack to a named classmate to hand over, not to the absent student. Catch-up becomes something a friend does with you, and the product records that it happened.

*This is a reframing candidate, not evidence. The synthesis agent decides whether it strengthens the strategic compound.*

## Research Synthesis

**Strategic connection:** Strong, and not where the seed put it. Return from absence is the first break No Lost Week names. The family-engagement connection is weak: Measure 2a counts message opt-ins.

**Market validation:** Strong. A dated Directive and 57 of 212 RFPs.

**Customer evidence:** Medium. The streams disagree about what solves the problem. EdTech SME reads a documented provision as the thing districts are buying. Educator SME says a provision the student never opens is paperwork. Both are right about their own customer. Do not average them.

**Competitive gap:** Partial. Corvid has the list; nobody has the assembled days. Corvid has announced for 2027.

**Revenue signal:** Medium. Renewals in districts whose RFPs name it; no separate price.

## Portfolio Context

### Related Ideas (header fields for differentiation)

**absence-alerts-for-families** (developing)

- Core insight: Families learn their child missed a lesson days later, from the child.
- Problem: Attendance is recorded by period and reported home by term.
- Who cares: Families, attendance leads.
- Strategic connection: Advances Measure 2a by giving families a message worth opting in to.

**lesson-recording-archive** (seed)

- Core insight: A lesson that was taught once cannot be seen again by anyone who was not in the room.
- Problem: Explanations given aloud are lost to absent students.
- Who cares: Teachers, absent students.
- Strategic connection: Supports No Lost Week by keeping the spoken part of a lesson.

**flexible-deadline-policy** (seed)

- Core insight: A due date in Coursebook is the same for a student who was present and one who was out all week.
- Problem: Teachers extend deadlines one student at a time.
- Who cares: Teachers, returning students.
- Strategic connection: Supports No Lost Week by moving deadlines when attendance says they should move.
