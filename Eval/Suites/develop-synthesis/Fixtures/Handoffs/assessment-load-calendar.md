# Research Handoff: assessment-load-calendar

**Date:** 2026-11-10
**Idea:** Assessment Load Calendar
**Seed core insight:** Every teacher sets Coursebook due dates alone, so a student can find five pieces of work due on one day and no adult saw it coming.
**Seed strategic connection:** Extends Fewer Tabs by showing a year group's deadlines in one view, using the school timetable Coursebook already holds.

---

## Strategic Context (Stream A)

### Product Strategy — None found

- Neither pillar addresses student workload. No Lost Week concerns broken routines; Entered Once concerns evidence.

### Design Strategy — Weak

- Fewer Tabs is about a teacher switching tools. The seed stretches it: a deadline view saves no teacher a tab. ([Larkmoor Design Strategy 2026, Fewer Tabs](https://example.com/larkmoor-design-strategy-2026-fewer-tabs))

### OKR Connections

- Measure 3b (students returning to Coursebook each week): Direct. The measure counts weekly returning students, and the one study on the subject finds they return less in the week after a pile-up (see Shared Research Baseline).

### NPS / Customer Evidence

- Larkmoor Coursebook, teachers and administrators (April–September 2026): Zero mentions of deadline clashes, workload, or due-date spread. ([Coursebook NPS — September 2026 tab](https://example.com/coursebook-nps))
- **Key finding:** Larkmoor surveys teachers and administrators. The people who feel a six-deadline Thursday are students, and Larkmoor does not ask them.

### Shared Research Baseline (within TTL)

- On days when a student has four or more deadlines, 34% of that work is handed in late, and the same students open their learning platform 22% less often in the following week. (medium; one study, 14 schools) ([Fernhollow Institute, Student Week Study 2025](https://example.com/fernhollow-student-week-2025))

## Market Intelligence (Stream B — EdTech SME)

### Market Fit: Weak

No buyer names it. None of the 212 basin district RFPs in the 2025–26 index mentions deadline spread or workload. Where a need shows, it shows in students organising themselves with a separate app.

### Competitive Positioning

| Competitor | Relevant Capability | Comparison |
| ------------ | --------------------- | ------------ |
| Quillfeather (discovered) | Standalone homework planner; 180,000 student accounts in 140 districts; students type in their own deadlines | Differentiation |
| Corvid Classroom (registry) | A student sees a calendar of their own deadlines; no view for staff across courses | Parity |
| Pennywhistle Assess (registry) | Nothing comparable | Differentiation |

**Positioning narrative:** No learning platform in the basin shows staff the combined deadline load on a class. Quillfeather's numbers say students want to see their week; they do not say a school will act on it. ([Quillfeather, About](https://example.com/quillfeather-about); [Kestrel Ridge Research, Classroom Tools Scan 2026](https://example.com/kestrel-ridge-classroom-tools-2026))

### Technology Risk: Medium

Coursebook holds every due date. It does not hold the school timetable or calendar: those live in the district's student information system, and the Open Rostering Profile brings across enrolments only. Exam weeks, trips, and report deadlines are invisible to Coursebook. Showing one teacher another teacher's deadlines is also a permissions change schools have not been asked about.

### Strategic Timing

No deadline, no announced competitor.

## Educator Perspective (Stream D)

### Adoption Assessment: Low

A teacher shown that Thursday is crowded will keep their Thursday. Their deadline was there first.

### Pain Point Validity

The pain is the student's. Teachers meet it second-hand, as late work and tired classes, and do not connect it to dates. No single teacher caused it and no single teacher can fix it.

### Adoption Barriers

Nobody owns the problem. The person who could act is a head of year, who sets no due dates and has no reason to open the screen where dates are set.

### Champion Scenario

A head of Year 10 sees, two weeks ahead, that the Thursday before reports has six deadlines across the year group, and asks two departments to move theirs.

## Divergent Reframing (Stream E)

### The Disruptive Angle: Students trade deadlines

Give each student a small number of two-day extensions a term to spend where the week is worst. The load is levelled by the people carrying it.

*This is a reframing candidate, not evidence. The synthesis agent decides whether it strengthens the strategic compound.*

## Research Synthesis

**Strategic connection:** Medium. No pillar covers workload, and the seed's Fewer Tabs connection does not hold. The connection that does hold is Measure 3b: pile-up days are followed by a week of lower return.

**Market validation:** Weak. Zero RFP mentions. Demand is visible only in Quillfeather's student adoption.

**Customer evidence:** Weak. Zero NPS mentions; the people who feel it are not surveyed. Educator lens rates adoption Low for teachers and names the head of year as the one person placed to act.

**Competitive gap:** Confirmed. No platform shows staff the combined load.

**Revenue signal:** Unknown. Market sizing unavailable.
