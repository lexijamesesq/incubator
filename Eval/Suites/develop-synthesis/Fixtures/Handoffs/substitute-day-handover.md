# Research Handoff: substitute-day-handover

**Date:** 2026-11-10
**Idea:** Substitute Day Handover
**Seed core insight:** A substitute teacher walks into a class with a paper folder, because Coursebook has no way to hand one day of one class to someone who is not on staff.
**Seed strategic connection:** Supports No Lost Week by keeping a lesson usable on a day its teacher is out.

---

## Strategic Context (Stream A)

### Product Strategy — Direct

- No Lost Week lists a teacher's absence as the second of its three breaks, after a student's absence and before a school closure. ([Larkmoor Product Strategy 2026, No Lost Week](https://example.com/larkmoor-product-strategy-2026-no-lost-week))

### Design Strategy — Direct

- Fewer Tabs: the teacher's sick-morning notes repeat what Coursebook already holds, written again in an email. ([Larkmoor Design Strategy 2026, Fewer Tabs](https://example.com/larkmoor-design-strategy-2026-fewer-tabs))

### OKR Connections

- Measure 3b (students returning to Coursebook each week): Adjacent. A covered lesson that runs in Coursebook keeps the class in it for that day.

### NPS / Customer Evidence

- Larkmoor Coursebook (April–September 2026): 12 teacher comments about writing cover notes, most of the form "I spend my sick morning writing sub notes". ([Coursebook NPS — September 2026 tab](https://example.com/coursebook-nps))
- **Key finding:** A small articulated signal from teachers. Nothing from a district office and nothing from a substitute, who is not a Coursebook user and is never surveyed.

### Shared Research Baseline (within TTL)

- A basin teacher is out a median 8 days a year, and on 61% of covered days the class does work unrelated to the current unit. (high) ([Fernhollow Institute, Cover Lesson Study 2025](https://example.com/fernhollow-cover-lesson-2025))
- A district staffing coordinator says about a third of cover days are booked the same morning. (low; single conversation, no published source)

## Market Intelligence (Stream B — EdTech SME)

### Market Fit: Weak

The person with the problem has no budget and the office with the budget has a different system. Districts book substitutes through a staffing office, on a staffing platform, from a staffing budget. None of the 212 basin district RFPs issued in 2025–26 for a learning platform mentions substitute access ([Ledgerline RFP Index 2026](https://example.com/ledgerline-rfp-index-2026)).

### Competitive Positioning

| Competitor | Relevant Capability | Comparison |
| ------------ | --------------------- | ------------ |
| Corvid Classroom (registry) | Guest access by email invitation; the guest sees the whole course for 30 days | Differentiation |
| Brindle Cover (discovered) | Staffing platform used to book substitutes; a free-text "notes for cover" field; no link to any learning platform | Differentiation |
| Pennywhistle Assess (registry) | Nothing comparable | Differentiation |

**Positioning narrative:** Nobody offers access scoped to one class for one day. Corvid's guest invitation is the nearest thing and it gives a stranger a month in the course. The gap is open because no learning-platform buyer has asked anyone to close it. ([Corvid Classroom help, Guest access](https://example.com/corvid-guest-access); [Brindle Cover product page](https://example.com/brindle-cover))

### Technology Risk: Medium

The plan itself is assembly of what is posted. The access is the hard part: a person with no district account, arriving fifteen minutes before the first period, on a phone. The Learner Data Charter requires that a non-staff adult sees no student record beyond names.

### Strategic Timing

No forcing function and no competitor moving. Nothing is lost by waiting a year, and nothing is gained by it.

## Market Sizing (Stream B — TAM Estimate)

### TAM / SAM / SOM

| Level | Moderate Estimate | Confidence |
| ------- | ------------------- | ------------ |
| TAM | $6M | Low |
| SAM | $2.5M | Low |
| SOM | $0.3M–$0.8M | Low |

### Key Assumptions

- The only related spend is the staffing platform fee, which Larkmoor does not compete for. The estimate assumes a learning platform could attach a small per-school add-on; no district has been asked.
- Larkmoor's contracts price per enrolled student. A substitute login is a user who pays nothing, so each one adds support cost and no seat revenue.

### Revenue-Potential Signal: Low

No buyer, no price, and a cost per login. If it earns anything it earns it as goodwill with teachers.

## Educator Perspective (Stream D)

### Adoption Assessment: High for the teacher, Medium for the substitute

A teacher will press one button from bed. A substitute will use it if it opens on a phone from a link with no password.

### Pain Point Validity

Felt sharply and rarely: eight mornings a year, each one while ill. The substitute's pain is constant and nobody hears it, because a substitute is in a different school every day.

### Adoption Barriers

Anything that needs an account. Anything the office has to approve that morning. Seating notes and the names of students who need watching are what a substitute wants most, and they are exactly what should not be on a stranger's phone.

### Champion Scenario

A substitute for a Year 7 science class opens a link at 8:15 and sees four periods in order: the room, the task already posted for each class, and where the class left off yesterday. Period two does the planned practical instead of a film.

## Divergent Reframing (Stream E)

### The Disruptive Angle: A substitute who keeps a record

If substitutes had a standing Larkmoor identity across districts, the product could show a school which substitutes ran the planned lesson. Cover becomes a rated service.

*This is a reframing candidate, not evidence. The synthesis agent decides whether it strengthens the strategic compound.*

## Research Synthesis

**Strategic connection:** Strong. A teacher's absence is a named break in No Lost Week, and Fewer Tabs describes the duplicated notes.

**Market validation:** Weak. Zero RFP mentions; the budget sits in a staffing office Larkmoor does not sell to.

**Customer evidence:** Medium. Twelve teacher comments; educator lens rates teacher adoption High. No evidence from substitutes.

**Competitive gap:** Confirmed, and unvalued. Nobody has built day-scoped access and nobody has been asked to.

**Revenue signal:** Weak. No buyer, and each substitute login costs support without adding a seat.
