## Absence Catch-Up Pack

**Core insight:** The attendance office knows who was out and Coursebook knows what each class posted, and because the two never meet, a student back from a week of flu has to ask six teachers what she missed.
**Problem it addresses:** Each teacher rebuilds a returning student's missed work by hand, from a paper tray of handouts or a feed message re-posted with the absent names at the top, and the student stops asking by the third teacher.
**Who cares:** Students back from a long absence and least likely to go looking, teachers who hear the same "what did I miss?" before the Monday bell, and district attendance leads who must document a continuity provision for every absence of three days or more from August 2027.
**Strategic connection:** Implements No Lost Week at the first break it names, a student's absence, by joining the attendance record to what each class posted so a student who misses the days does not also lose the week after them.

### Research Summary

- Teachers spend a median 25 minutes a week per absent student assembling missed work, and 38% of students absent three days or more never complete it. ([Fernhollow Institute, Absence and Recovery Study 2025](https://example.com/fernhollow-absence-recovery-2025))
- From August 2027 the Attendance Recovery Directive requires a district to document the learning continuity provision made for every absence of three days or more. ([Basin Education Authority, Attendance Recovery Directive guidance](https://example.com/bea-attendance-recovery-directive))
- 57 of 212 basin district RFPs issued in 2025–26 ask for absence-continuity features. ([Ledgerline RFP Index 2026](https://example.com/ledgerline-rfp-index-2026))
- Corvid Classroom's product lead said in an October 2026 webinar that catch-up bundles are planned for 2027. (Corvid Classroom product webinar, October 2026)
- Coursebook's April–September 2026 NPS comments mention absence or catch-up zero times, while 31 teacher comments describe re-posting instructions "for the kids who were out". ([Coursebook NPS — September 2026 tab](https://example.com/coursebook-nps))

### Thought Outline

The move is to put a returning student's missed days on one page before she reaches the first desk. The attendance office knows who was out and Coursebook holds what each class posted, so Coursebook can gather those days by subject and have the page waiting on her first morning back. That gives her one place to start on the day she is most behind while sparing her teachers a rebuild by hand while giving the district's attendance lead a dated record of the provision made for each absence the Attendance Recovery Directive covers — one move serving student, teacher and district simultaneously. The record exists the moment the pack does, but only her use turns it from paperwork into provision. Nobody has shown yet that a fourteen-year-old back from a week of flu will open it, which is why she, not the district, is the customer to design for. Coursebook knows what was posted, not which items mattered, so the open decision is how much to ask of the teacher: the pack works only if someone marks the two or three essentials, a new step on a morning she is already behind. The window is about nine months, before the Directive applies and Corvid ships its bundles.

### Open Questions

- Would a fourteen-year-old open the pack if we built it?
- Will teachers mark the two or three items that matter on a morning they are already behind?
- Does a pack Coursebook assembles count as the learning continuity provision the Attendance Recovery Directive asks districts to document?
- Can the pack name the days a student missed without revealing why she was out?
- Would a pack handed over by a named classmate be opened when one sent to the student is not?

### Original Capture

From the advisory session. A Year 9 form tutor described Monday mornings: three students back from a week of flu, each one asking six teachers what they missed. She keeps a paper tray labelled with the days of the week and puts a copy of every handout in it. Another teacher re-posts the week's instructions in the course feed with the absent students' names at the top. One parent emailed all six teachers separately. Could Coursebook put together what a class did on the days a student was out? Would a fourteen-year-old open it if we did? Do we know how much teacher time goes into this each week? The attendance office knows who was out and Coursebook knows what was posted, and the two never meet.
