## Conference Evidence Sheet

**Core insight:** Every result a teacher pastes into her conference notes is already in Benchmark, and Benchmark still cannot give her one page about one student that she can edit and bring to the conversation.
**Problem it addresses:** Before each conference round a teacher opens the Benchmark tracker for every student in turn, screenshots the row, pastes it into a document and types two sentences beneath it.
**Who cares:** Teachers who lose hours to screenshots before every conference round, families who get two conversations a year about their child's results, and districts deciding whether Benchmark still earns its place beside Coursebook now that Corvid prints a sheet.
**Strategic connection:** Implements Entered Once at the parent conference, the occasion the pillar names as its most visible failure, by putting the account Benchmark already holds onto one page per student, and supports Measure 1c by removing a reason districts gave for dropping Benchmark.

### Research Summary

- Basin teachers spend a median 4.5 hours preparing for each conference round, and there are two rounds a year, in October and March. ([Fernhollow Institute, Teacher Time Study 2025](https://example.com/fernhollow-teacher-time-study-2025))
- Corvid Classroom shipped Conference Cards in March 2026: a one-page summary per student, translated into six home languages. ([Corvid Classroom release notes, March 2026](https://example.com/corvid-release-notes-2026-03))
- Pennywhistle Assess offers a class-level report only, with no per-student page, and Mossgate Labs has nothing comparable. (EdTech SME market intelligence, November 2026)
- Benchmark's NPS drew 27 comments about conference preparation between April and September 2026, most describing screenshots pasted into a document. ([Benchmark NPS — September 2026 tab](https://example.com/benchmark-nps))
- Nine districts dropped Benchmark and kept Coursebook in 2025–26, and two of them cited parent reporting in exit interviews. ([Larkmoor exit interviews 2025–26](https://example.com/larkmoor-exit-interviews-2026))

### Thought Outline

The move is to have Benchmark draft a one-page sheet for each student, with the term's standards and the latest result beside each, and to let the teacher edit it before she prints. Every figure on the page is already in Benchmark, so the work is layout, print and translation. The edit is what gets the sheet used, because a teacher will not hand a parent a page she cannot change. A draft the teacher corrects turns what took one teacher two weekends into an evening of edits while giving districts that weigh parent reporting a reason to keep Benchmark beside Coursebook while closing the gap Corvid opened in March — one move answering the teacher, the district and Corvid at once. The plain-language term report and the absence alerts reach families too, but neither gives the teacher a page for the conversation across the table. This is a catch-up move, and its case is teacher time and renewals, not a lead. The clock is the school calendar: a sheet that misses the October 2027 round leaves teachers screenshotting for another year while Corvid's customers print theirs.

### Open Questions

- Does the page show every standard, or only the three that matter this term?
- Does the sheet go home with the family, or stay in the room?
- Does the sheet need translating into families' home languages to stand level with Corvid's Conference Cards?
- Do the two exit interviews that cited parent reporting speak for the other seven districts that dropped Benchmark?
- Should the student choose what goes on the sheet and present it at the conference?
- What is the smallest version of the sheet teachers can use in the October 2027 round?
