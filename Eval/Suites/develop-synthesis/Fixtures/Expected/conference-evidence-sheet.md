## Conference Evidence Sheet

**Core insight:** Teachers rebuild each student's Benchmark results by hand before every parent conference, because Benchmark holds every result and has no page about one student that a parent can hold.
**Problem it addresses:** Twice a year a teacher screenshots one tracker row per student, pastes it into a document and types the context underneath, spending hours on evidence the product already has.
**Who cares:** Teachers who give up weekends to conference preparation, families who leave the conference with no single account of their child, district leaders weighing whether to keep Benchmark alongside Coursebook.
**Strategic connection:** Implements Entered Once on the occasion the pillar itself names as the place today's record fails most visibly, by making one student's Benchmark evidence read as one account a parent can hold; it supports Measure 1c, and that link is thin.

### Research Summary

- Corvid Classroom shipped Conference Cards in March 2026, a one-page summary per student translated into six home languages, which makes the seed's claim that no platform produces a conference-ready sheet wrong. ([Corvid Classroom release notes, March 2026](https://example.com/corvid-release-notes-2026-03))
- Basin teachers spend a median 4.5 hours preparing for each conference round, and there are two rounds a year, in October and March. ([Fernhollow Institute, Teacher Time Study 2025](https://example.com/fernhollow-teacher-time-study-2025))
- Pennywhistle Assess offers a class-level report with no per-student page and Mossgate Labs has nothing comparable, so the gap sits with Corvid alone. (Stream B competitive registry, research handoff)
- Benchmark drew 27 comments about conference preparation between April and September 2026, most describing screenshots pasted into a document and clustered around the two conference rounds. ([Benchmark NPS — September 2026 tab](https://example.com/benchmark-nps))
- Nine districts dropped Benchmark and kept Coursebook in 2025–26, and two of them cited parent reporting in exit interviews. ([Larkmoor exit interviews 2025–26](https://example.com/larkmoor-exit-interviews-2026))

### Thought Outline

This is a catch-up move, and the case for it is teacher time. Build one editable page per student from results Benchmark already holds, ready for the October 2027 conference round. The sheet makes Benchmark the place teachers prepare conferences while putting one readable account of a child in front of every family while taking parent reporting out of the reasons a district gives for dropping Benchmark — one move serving adoption, competitive parity and renewal simultaneously. Each outcome depends on the one before it. Teachers hand a parent only a sheet they can edit, so editing decides adoption. Adoption decides whether families ever see Benchmark's evidence as one account, which buyers now expect an assessment product to provide and Corvid's customers already have. A district keeps Benchmark for its family reporting only when that reporting reaches the families in its own classrooms. The last link is the weakest: two exiting districts of nine cited parent reporting, so renewal is the outcome this bet has to prove, not the one it starts from.

### Open Questions

- What goes on the page: every standard, or the three that matter this term?
- Does the sheet go home, or stay in the room?
- Does parent reporting drive Benchmark drops beyond the two exit interviews that cite it?
- Can translation into home languages ship in time for the October 2027 round?
- Does the student choose what goes on the sheet and lead the conference?
