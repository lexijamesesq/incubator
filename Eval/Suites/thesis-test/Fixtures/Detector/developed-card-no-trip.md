## Absence Catch-Up Pack

**Core insight:** A student back from a week out queues at six desks to find out what they missed, because the attendance record knows who was out, Coursebook knows what was posted, and the two never meet.
**Problem it addresses:** Every teacher rebuilds the missed work by hand for every absence, with a paper tray of handouts or a re-posted feed message, and the student stops asking by the third teacher.
**Who cares:** Returning students who lose the week and not the day, teachers who field the same "what did I miss?" before the bell, district attendance leads who must document a learning continuity provision for every absence of three days or more from August 2027, and parents who email six teachers to get one list.
**Strategic connection:** Advances No Lost Week, which names a student's return from absence as the first break to fix, by joining the attendance record to what each class posted so the learner loses the day and not the week.

### Research Summary

- Teachers spend a median 25 minutes a week per absent student assembling missed work, and 38% of students absent three days or more never complete it. ([Fernhollow Institute, Absence and Recovery Study 2025](https://example.com/fernhollow-absence-recovery-2025))
- The Attendance Recovery Directive takes effect August 2027 and requires a district to document the "learning continuity provision" made for every absence of three days or more. ([Basin Education Authority, Attendance Recovery Directive guidance](https://example.com/bea-attendance-recovery-directive))
- 57 of 212 basin district RFPs issued in 2025–26 ask for absence-continuity features. ([Ledgerline RFP Index 2026](https://example.com/ledgerline-rfp-index-2026))
- Corvid Classroom shipped a "Missed Work" filter in September 2025 that lists a student's unsubmitted assignments by date with no lesson content, and its product lead said "catch-up bundles" are planned for 2027. (Corvid product lead, October 2026 webinar, no public link)
- Coursebook NPS from April to September 2026 has zero mentions of absence or catch-up, while 31 teacher comments describe re-posting instructions "for the kids who were out". ([Coursebook NPS — September 2026 tab](https://example.com/coursebook-nps))

### Thought Outline

The move is to join two records Larkmoor already holds, who was out and what each class posted, into one page per returning student, with each teacher marking the two or three items that matter. That page answers the district's absence-continuity RFP while it brings the returning student back into Coursebook on the first day back while it takes Larkmoor past Corvid's list before Corvid's 2027 bundles arrive—one move serving renewal revenue, weekly engagement and competitive position simultaneously. The returns build on each other in one direction the research supports. The teacher's marking makes the page worth opening, each open is a student returning to Coursebook that week, and those opens are what separate a provision from paperwork. The renewal case stands on the documented page alone, because the document is what districts are buying. The research does not show that a used provision renews better than a filed one, and it does not show that a fourteen-year-old opens the page, so the engagement return and the link from engagement back to revenue are still bets.

### Open Questions

- Would a returning fourteen-year-old open the pack?
- Will teachers mark the two or three essential items on a day they are already behind?
- Does a district renew on a documented provision alone, or on one its students used?
- Can a pack show what was missed without revealing why the student was out, as the Learner Data Charter requires?
- Does handing the pack to a named classmate get it opened more often than giving it to the absent student?

### Original Capture

From the advisory session. A Year 9 form tutor described Monday mornings: three students back from a week of flu, each one asking six teachers what they missed. She keeps a paper tray labelled with the days of the week and puts a copy of every handout in it. Another teacher re-posts the week's instructions in the course feed with the absent students' names at the top. One parent emailed all six teachers separately. Could Coursebook put together what a class did on the days a student was out? Would a fourteen-year-old open it if we did? Do we know how much teacher time goes into this each week? The attendance office knows who was out and Coursebook knows what was posted, and the two never meet.
