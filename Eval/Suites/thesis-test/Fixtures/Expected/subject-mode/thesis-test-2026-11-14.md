## Recomposition: instructor-led dual enrolment

No route survives as written. The parts that survived are put together:

- **From C, the door.** Dual-enrolment courses in districts already on Coursebook, in the partnerships whose agreement leaves the platform open.
- **From B, the motion.** The instructor adopts Coursebook for one course of their own, and nobody at the college buys anything. B's educator and learning-outcome cells held; what broke in B was the step from use to purchase.
- **From A's break, what not to lead with.** The office-first entry is closed, and it led with Benchmark. The recomposed route leads with Coursebook and with teaching.

**Recomposed position:** Larkmoor enters regional colleges one dual-enrolment course at a time. The partner district's Coursebook licence covers the course, the college instructor builds it there, and the district, not the instructor, introduces Larkmoor to the college's technology office when the partnership renews.

Evidence: edtech-sme found 29 of 38 published partnership agreements leave the platform open. Interpretation: the route starts in those 29 and not in the segment at large, because in the other 9 the district's Coursebook is not permitted to be where the course runs.

Evidence: educator-sme found 64% of instructors built a new course in a tool they chose and 9% moved an old one. Interpretation: the first course must be one that is new to the instructor, which a dual-enrolment course for school students usually is, because asking them to move an established course fails nine times in ten.

Evidence: tam-estimate found 3 of 61 college purchases followed instructor adoption, and Corvid converted four colleges in two years. Interpretation: instructor use is treated as the proof and not as the sale, because the purchase is made by a technology office that needs a reason from a peer institution; the partner district supplies it.

Evidence: edtech-sme found 388 of 412 colleges take a placement test at no cost. Interpretation: Benchmark is left out of the entry altogether, because the one office that would buy testing already has it for nothing.

### Conditions under which it holds

1. If interviews with at least ten dual-enrolment instructors by March 2027 show they will not teach in a district's Coursebook, kill. This is the UNKNOWN cell and it is resolved before any build.
2. If fewer than 15 dual-enrolment courses are running in Coursebook by the end of the 2027 intake term, kill.
3. If no college technology office has agreed to a meeting through a partner district by June 2028, kill the route to purchase and keep the courses.
4. If the share of published agreements requiring the college's platform rises above half at the 2028 renewal, kill.

## Premortem

It failed, and this is why. The instructors were never asked. Larkmoor provisioned the courses through the districts in August 2027, and most college instructors taught from their own college platform and posted a link, because logging in to a school system felt like working for the school. The few who did build in Coursebook liked it and told nobody with a budget. The districts, asked to make introductions, had no reason to spend their goodwill on a vendor's sales call. At the 2028 renewal, three more colleges wrote their own platform into the agreement, and the route closed from the college side.
