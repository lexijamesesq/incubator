## Accommodations That Apply Themselves

**Core insight:** A teacher setting a Benchmark classroom check has to remember which students get extra time or a read-aloud and switch it on for each one, because the support plan that says so lives in a system Benchmark never sees.
**Problem it addresses:** A student with a documented accommodation sits a check without it whenever a busy teacher forgets, and nobody can show afterwards whether it was applied.
**Who cares:** Teachers with several supported students in every class, district learning-support coordinators who are audited under the Learner Support Statute, and the students whose results understate what they know.
**Strategic connection:** Implements Entered Once by making a student's accommodations part of the record Benchmark reads at the moment a check is assigned.

### Opportunity Assessment

| Dimension | Rating | Rationale |
| ----------- | -------- | ----------- |
| Customer sentiment | High | Teachers admit to forgetting and coordinators admit they cannot prove otherwise. |
| User experience | Medium | Setting a check stays the same task with one worry removed from it. |
| Revenue potential | Medium | Coordinators want the audit record, and whether they hold a budget for it is unknown. |
| Industry disruption | Medium | Competitors offer manual settings, and none applies a student's plan without the teacher. |
| Strategic alignment | High | An accommodation that follows the student is one record per learner in its plainest form. |

### Research Summary

- From January 2027, a district must show that each documented accommodation was applied on each assessed task. ([Basin Education Authority, Learner Support Statute audit guidance, April 2026](https://example.com/bea-learner-support-audit-guidance))
- Tamarisk Plans holds the support plans of 78% of basin districts and offers no export interface. ([Kestrel Ridge Research, Learner Support Systems Scan, January 2024](https://example.com/kestrel-ridge-learner-support-2024))
- Corvid Classroom added a profile-level extra-time setting in September 2025; Pennywhistle Assess sets accommodations by hand on each assessment. ([Corvid Classroom release notes, September 2025](https://example.com/corvid-release-notes-2025-09))
- 19 of 22 learning-support coordinators interviewed could not evidence that accommodations were applied; 26 of 34 teachers had forgotten one at least once that term. ([Larkmoor district interviews, June 2026](https://example.com/larkmoor-district-interviews-2026-06))
- Support Flags, Larkmoor's 2024 pilot that showed an icon beside supported students, was retired after teachers in 9 of 12 pilot schools said they had stopped noticing it. ([Support Flags retrospective, December 2024](https://example.com/support-flags-retrospective))

### Thought Outline

The move is to stop asking the teacher to remember. A student's accommodations are entered once and Benchmark applies them to every check that student is assigned: the timer is longer, the read-aloud is on, and the record says so. That gives the student the conditions their plan promises while taking a per-check chore off the teacher while handing the coordinator an audit trail nobody had to compile — one move serving the learner, the classroom, and the compliance office together. Support Flags failed because it informed the teacher and left the work with them; this does the work. The open decision is where the accommodations come from: read from the plan system, or entered in Benchmark.

### Open Questions

- Will Tamarisk Plans release plan data to another product?
- Do teachers trust an accommodation they did not switch on themselves?
- Does a coordinator hold a budget, or only an obligation?
- Do regional colleges' disability services offices, which keep similar plans, want this at all?

### Original Capture

Notes from the September renewals review. Two coordinators said the same thing: the Statute audit starts in January and they have no way to show a child got their extra time on a Tuesday quiz. Teachers set it by hand in Benchmark, one student and one check at a time, and they forget. The plans themselves are in Tamarisk in most districts. For scale: Benchmark is in 427 of the basin's 1,940 districts (22%) per the Basin Schools Register 2025, about 11% of basin students have a support plan per the same register, and Coursebook is in 12% of the 412 regional colleges, where disability services keep plans too but nobody on our side has spoken to one. Somebody at the Buyers' Roundtable said districts spend about sixty dollars a year per supported student on compliance paperwork. I have no source for that and would not build on it. We tried something here before, Support Flags, and it changed nothing because it was only an icon. Is this something a coordinator buys, or something a teacher just expects to work?
